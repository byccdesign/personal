An interested lead had no simple path to a proposal

Opportunity

Insurance was interesting to many people, but it was not always a priority and it could be difficult to know where to start. Advisors often relied on personal networks, referrals, social channels, spreadsheets, and separate portals to manage the relationship.

Mobile was the working environment

In focus groups, 80% of advisors said they accessed the legacy experience on a tablet or phone, where layouts and field validation made data entry harder.

Client records were maintained twice

85% said they used Excel or other external tools to manage clients, then manually entered the information into the internal CRM.

Comparison took too many attempts

Advisors created multiple dummy proposals to show options, making it difficult for clients to compare price and benefits in a meeting.

New leads needed a low-pressure start

People could be interested in insurance without knowing an advisor or feeling ready for a sales conversation.

One journey, four clear stages

Quick Quote

Self-service entry

A useful first step, even before talking to an advisor

We created Quick Quote as a separate page under the EWA demo so people arriving from the website could explore their coverage goals and a possible monthly allocation without first navigating the advisor platform.

The short entry collected only what was needed to begin: coverage goals such as family protection, education, retirement, savings, or investments; monthly allocation; date of birth; gender; name; and an email address for the quote.

A lead profile built for the next decision

Advisor workflow

Capture information once and carry it into the proposal

When someone wanted to learn more, the advisor created a lead profile with basic identity and contact details, employment and source-of-funds information, and the information needed for underwriting. Inline validation and clearer field guidance were designed to reduce avoidable errors and duplicate records.

The profile also supported different policy relationships: the customer record could connect to multiple policies, and each policy could have a different owner and insured person, with its own riders and beneficiaries. Keeping those relationships at the policy level made the data model reflect real family and coverage arrangements.

The questionnaire became a guided journey

Interaction design

Long, conditional questions need their own space

The underwriting and suitability questions gathered health, financial, existing-coverage, and needs information. Putting that much conditional content in a modal would hide context, make progress hard to understand, and create a poor experience on the tablets advisors use in the field.

Use a full-page, step-by-step flow

Break the questionnaire into short sections with a visible progress indicator and clear section names. Keep one decision group in focus at a time.

Reveal relevant questions progressively

Ask follow-up questions only when an answer makes them relevant, such as provider and coverage details after a lead reports existing insurance.

Explain sensitive questions in place

Use plain-language help beside financial, health, and identity questions, so an advisor can explain why information is needed without interrupting the flow.

Make review and correction easy

Validate fields inline, preserve completed answers when moving between sections, and provide a final review before the assessment is submitted.

Why not a modal? A dedicated page supports long forms, conditional branches, keyboard and screen-reader navigation, and tablet-sized content. A modal is better reserved for a short confirmation or a focused, optional lookup.

Needs calculations, including estate planning and income protection, should show which inputs contribute to each result and distinguish calculated values from information supplied by the customer. A risk profile assessment remains a required step even when investment is not one of the stated goals.

Compare products in one conversation

Advisor and client

Keep the chosen product visible while comparing two alternatives

The comparison step uses three aligned columns: the selected product and two alternatives. Advisors can change either comparison product without losing the original selection, then review price and benefits in the same view.

Consistent information order makes differences easier to scan. Each product presents its premium and core plan details first, with benefits and riders available in a predictable place. This replaces the meeting-time workaround of creating separate dummy proposals.

Turn the selected plan into a shareable illustration

Proposal handoff

Continue from comparison into a useful proposal

Once the client selected a product, the advisor could generate a sales illustration from the collected profile and selected plan. The output was designed to support the next conversation, whether it happened in person or remotely.

Email

Send the illustration to the email address provided by the client.

Download

Download a PDF for manual sharing or offline review.

Remote access

Generate a unique link so the client can sign in and review the proposal outside the meeting.

Keep context connected

Retain the selected product and lead record so the advisor can continue follow-up from the CRM.

Strategy shaped in the details

As UI/UX Manager and lead product designer, I translated the broader transformation into an end-to-end proposal experience while staying hands-on across research, workflow definition, interaction design, and prototyping.

Designed for two advisor channels

The same journey needed to work for Bancassurance advisers in bank branches and Agency financial advisers working through personal networks and referrals.

Connected the demo to the CRM

Quick Quote could begin on the public website, while interested leads and ongoing advisor work connected back to the new CRM for tracking and follow-up.

Reduced complexity without losing necessary detail

Progressive data capture and policy-level relationships kept a complex insurance application understandable while preserving the information needed to build and compare a proposal.

Takeaway

A proposal workflow is more than a form and a price calculation. It is how an advisor learns what matters, helps a client understand their options, and carries the conversation forward. Connecting those steps made the work clearer for both sides.
