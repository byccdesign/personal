Core and Growth

Freelancer.com

Global marketplace (2018 - 2020)

I worked across product teams in Manila, Sydney, Vancouver, and London to improve the experiences that help freelancers build trust and employers find and hire the right talent. The work spanned three connected funnels, each with its own users, constraints, and measures of success.

Scope

Freelancer onboarding and KYC · Employer search and discovery · Hiring and project setup

Role

Product Designer

Platform

Team

Product Managers, Engineers, QA, Front-end Engineers, and Data Scientists across Growth, Core, Search, Messaging, Client Success, and Trust & Safety Teams.

Challenge

Context

Three moments in the marketplace journey shaped the growth opportunity.

01 · Establish trust

Freelancers needed to create credible profiles and complete the identity checks required to participate in the marketplace.

02 · Find a match

Employers often knew the outcome they wanted, but not the skills or keywords needed to find the right freelancer.

03 · Projects setup

Employers needed to evaluate proposals, understand milestones, and choose a hiring model that fit the project.

Process

Cross-functional Collaboration

I mapped the connected journey, then investigated each funnel on its own terms.

Working with product managers, engineering, QA, and data scientists, I looked at where members dropped off, disengaged, struggled to verify their accounts, or had difficulty finding relevant talent.

Embedded with product teams, I contributed to end-to-end experiences across freelancer onboarding, employer discovery, and core hiring.

Current Search Behaviour

Search funnel

Three separate search experiences made discovery fragmented.

  • Employers moved between different pages to find freelancers, jobs, and contests.
  • Inconsistent patterns and ranking behaviour made it harder to compare results and understand which path would help them find the right match.

UX Research

Search Funnel

The freelancer-search task took six minutes in our study.

We tested two starting points. Participants were asked to find a part-time freelancer for a project such as creating a brochure. The study covered both desktop and mobile search experiences.

What search behaviour revealed

  • In search-call data, 17% included project keywords.
  • Among those keyword searches, 84% also included skills, suggesting employers often translated a project need into platform terminology.
  • Employers could find freelancers but lacked enough relevant information to confidently compare fit.
  • Newly registered and verified freelancers were not consistently visible in results.

Solutions

3 funnel workstreams

Three workstreams addressed three different points in the journey.

The sections below cover onboarding and KYC, employer search and discovery, and hiring setup. They connect as one marketplace journey, while each has a distinct user need and outcome.

One marketplace journey, with different needs at each step.

01 - Onboarding and KYC

Establish trust and credibility

Made identity verification feel like building a trusted profile, not clearing a hurdle

Freelancers had to build a credible profile and pass identity checks to take part in the marketplace, with different needs for individual and corporate accounts. In Australia, California, and the EU, payment rules added further verification steps.

Design decision

Connect account type, skills, profile details, linked accounts, and identity checks into one setup journey, and explain each verification requirement as part of building a trusted profile instead of a separate gate.

Payment verification requirements

For users in Australia, California, and the EU, payment requirements could add verification steps beyond profile setup. Depending on the provider and applicable policy, employers might need individual or business verification before funding a project, while freelancers might need identity and payout-account checks before withdrawing funds. Business verification could also require entity or beneficial-owner details.

Result

Verified accounts increased by 35% over a 3-month period across the onboarding workstream. This is a team outcome, not a result attributed to a single screen.

02 - Find a match

Search a Project, or Contest, Find Talent/Freelancer

Explored one search experience that starts from what employers actually type

Employers moved between three separate search experiences for freelancers, jobs, and contests. In our study, finding a freelancer took 6 minutes. Search data showed only 17% of searches included project keywords, and 84% of those also added skills, so employers were translating their project into platform terms. New verified freelancers also weren’t consistently visible in results.

Design exploration

I explored a unified search across all three categories that kept the skills-led path for employers who knew what to search for, and added broader intent, recommendations, and filters for those who didn’t. I also concepted community pages that show portfolio work next to skills, location, rate, and reputation, so employers could judge fit beyond keywords.

Result

This was a design exploration, so I don’t claim results for it alone. Across the broader Growth, Core, and Search work, profile engagement rose 11% and portfolio engagement rose 8%.

03 - Projects Setup

Hiring Experience

Clarified how employers choose between hourly and fixed-price hiring

Employers had to compare proposals and decide how to structure a project, but unclear project type, budget, and hourly versus fixed-price terms made it harder to get started and manage milestones.

Design decision

Make project type and budget clear up front, and show the difference between fixed-price and hourly work before the employer begins.

My contribution

I contributed to the hiring path from proposal comparison into project setup, helping clarify the decisions employers needed to make.

Further opportunity explored

The journey map also surfaced opportunities after the hire: clearer verification steps at funding and payout, project management, milestone payments, time tracking, and two-way feedback. These are future directions, not outcomes claimed for the delivered work.

For a future iteration, I would track verification completion and time-to-verify alongside funded-project completion, payout success, payment holds, and verification-related support contacts.

Result

Across related hiring work, hiring support tickets dropped 35% and hourly-hiring funnel revenue rose 60% within 6 months.

Learnings

Let employers describe the job before asking for the skill.

Service marketplaces should bridge plain-language needs to freelancer categories and specialties, then let employers refine their search.

Prove relevance in the result.

Skills and reputation are useful signals; portfolios and recent work help employers understand what a freelancer can actually deliver.

Trust is part of the marketplace journey.

Freelancer onboarding, verification, discovery, and hiring influence each other. Measure each intervention clearly, then use the full journey to find where confidence and conversion break down.
