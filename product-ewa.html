Challenge

Context

One ecosystem, different users

Each platform served a different part of the insurance journey from agents and branch teams managing sales, to operations teams processing policies, to customers managing their coverage.

We needed to design each experience around its users' specific needs while creating a consistent, intuitive system across the entire ecosystem.

Balancing legacy improvements with future platform strategy

The core challenge was not only designing better screens, but deciding where design effort would create the most impact.

Strategic planning and execution

Our team had to balance legacy improvements with discovery, prototyping, usability testing, and the broader design thinking process behind the new platforms. This meant improving today’s workflows while shaping a more scalable end-to-end experience for advisors, customers, operations, and back-office teams.

Turning north star into focused design team workstreams

I translated the broader product direction into focused design workstreams for the Product Design team, assigning responsibilities across platforms while keeping everyone aligned to the same north star.

EWA Product Assets

Findings

Key Insights

The bottleneck was not only in the interface

We found that the biggest pain points were not the interface, but in the handoffs between systems and teams. The fragmented ecosystem created friction for advisors, customers, and operations teams, slowing down the entire insurance journey.

Advisors managed systems instead of customers

Fragmented tools made routine sales work slower and harder to track.

Data quality shaped underwriting speed

Missing or repeated information created downstream review and correction work.

Product configuration slowed time to market (4 months)

Creating or updating products required costly configuration within SAP. Each change added maintenance overhead, making itharder to launch new offerings and adapt quickly to changing business and customer needs.

Payment friction put policies at risk of lapsing

Digital payment options were not consistently available within the customer portal. Customers often had to switch between channels to pay their premiums, increasing friction and the risk of missed payments or policy lapses.

Aligning product design priorities around a shared north star

With the product design team assigned across multiple platforms and workstreams, alignment became essential. I helped anchor our design priorities around a shared north star: creating a more connected, scalable, and intuitive insurance experience across the ecosystem.

This helped us balance legacy improvements with discovery, testing, and new platform design while keeping each team’s work moving in the same direction.

Process

Cross-functional collaboration

Finding the gaps between systems and teams

Through interviews, focus groups, remote usability testing, and in-branch observation, we studied how advisors, customers, and operations teams moved through the insurance journey. The end-to-end mapping surfaced the real source of friction: paper forms, spreadsheets, repeated data entry, and unclear ownership between systems and teams.

Tools Used

Iterative Process

We worked in short, collaborative design sprints within an agile development cycle, moving from research and framing to prototyping, testing, and refinement. Each sprint turned feedback into focused improvements while keeping design, product, and engineering aligned.

Stakeholder alignment around product direction

I worked closely with Product Owners, QA, engineers, business analysts, sales, and department heads to translate the broader product direction into clear product design priorities.

I also helped build buy-in from senior stakeholders, including the CEO, VP of Sales, and Head of Digital Transformation, by connecting design decisions to business goals, operational needs, and customer outcomes.

Within the 1st year, we moved from research and ecosystem mapping to validated designs and production-ready experiences across multiple platforms.

Learnings

Digital transformation is not just translating paper forms to digital

The biggest opportunities came from redesigning operational workflows, handoffs, and business processes behind the interface rather than the screens themselves.

Cross-functional alignment is as important as design execution

Early collaboration with executives, operations, product, engineering, compliance, and external consultants reduced rework and accelerated decision-making throughout the program.

Designing for the whole journey, not only screens

Improving one touchpoint in isolation would have created downstream bottlenecks. Mapping the relationships between CRM, underwriting, onboarding, and back-office systems was critical to delivering meaningful impact.

Small data quality improvements can create significant business value

Reducing manual entry, improving form validation, and increasing visibility into application statuses had a direct impact on underwriting speed and policy issuance outcomes.

Building organizational design maturity scales impact beyond a single project

Introducing design systems, design reviews, Figma workflows, and design sprint practices helped create a foundation that future teams could build upon long after the transformation was completed.
