Eastwest Ageas

Financial Services / Insurance (2023-2024)

EastWest Ageas operated across multiple digital platforms supporting different stages of the insurance journey. As the business expanded its digital capabilities, independently developed systems created fragmented workflows for customers, financial advisors, and internal operations teams.

About

Context

EastWest Ageas is a life insurance firm formed in 2015 by EastWest Bank, one of the largest banks in the Philippines, and Ageas Group, a multinational insurance company headquartered in Brussels, operates in 13 countries worldwide.

Challenge

Problem

One ecosystem. Multiple disconnected experiences.

Each platform supported a different part of the insurance journey from agents and branch teams managing sales, to operations teams processing policies, to customers managing their coverage easily.

These experiences had evolved across different teams and systems, creating different workflows, interaction patterns, and operational dependencies.

We needed to design around the specific needs of each user group while creating a more connected and consistent experience across the ecosystem.

Main bottleneck

Information was fragmented. Financial advisors relied on manual processes, spreadsheets, repeated data entry, and handoffs between applications. What should have been one continuous insurance journey often felt like several disconnected processes.

Balancing legacy improvements with future platform strategy

  1. Improve existing, business-critical workflows without disrupting operations.
  2. Shape a more scalable experience for future platforms.

Research & Alignment

Cross-functional collaboration

Stakeholder alignment around product direction

I worked closely with Product Owners, QA, engineers, business analysts, sales, and department heads to translate the broader product direction into clear product design priorities.

Iterative Process

We worked in short, collaborative design sprints within an agile development cycle, moving from research and framing to prototyping, testing, and refinement. Each sprint turned feedback into focused improvements while keeping design, product, and engineering aligned.

I also helped build buy-in from senior stakeholders, including the CEO, VP of Sales, and Head of Digital Transformation, by connecting design decisions to business goals, operational needs, and customer outcomes.

Findings

Key Insights

The biggest friction wasn't within the interfaces. It was between systems and teams.

Through interviews, focus groups, remote usability testing, and in-branch observations, we examined how customers, advisors, and operations teams moved through the insurance journey.

Tools Used

01 - Fragmented handoffs slowed down the insurance journey

Customer information moved between disconnected platforms, creating repeated data entry, manual coordination, and unclear ownership between teams. The friction wasn't isolated to a single screen. It accumulated across the journey.

02 - Advisors managed systems instead of customers

Financial advisors navigated multiple tools to manage prospects, complete applications, compare products, and follow up with customers. The fragmented workflow made routine sales activities harder to manage and track.

03 - Legacy product configuration limited business agility (4 months time to market)

Creating or updating insurance products required costly configuration within SAP. These dependencies increased maintenance overhead and made it harder to introduce new offerings efficiently.

The opportunity was to improve the entire operational journey, not simply digitize existing forms.

A fragmented insurance ecosystem was slowing down both customers, advisors, and internal teams.

Making the strategic decision

Strategy

How do we deliver given the time constraints and resources available as we migrate our tools, improve design and development process?

We needed to balance immediate usability improvements with long-term platform strategy.

We considered three options for improving the ecosystem:

01 – Evidence

Address immediate usability and operational problems within the current platforms. Fastest to ship, but it leaves the root causes in place: fragmented handoffs and costly SAP configuration.

02 – Rebuild

Replace the underlying systems in one program. Cleanest end state, but the highest risk to business-critical workflows and a long wait before anyone sees value.

03 – Modernize Progressively

Improve critical workflows while introducing new platform experiences and progressively replacing legacy components.

We chose to modernize progressively.

We focused on the most critical workflows while introducing new platform experiences and progressively replacing legacy components. This approach allowed us to improve usability and operational efficiency without disrupting business-critical processes.

Transforming the Insurance Journey

Solution

Connecting customer acquisition, sales, operations, and policy servicing.

The transformation involved multiple interconnected platforms rather than a single application redesign. I led the design direction across the ecosystem and remained hands-on in key workflows, working with product, engineering, QA, sales, and operations to translate the broader strategy into production-ready experiences.

Remote Selling & Digital Lead Acquisition

Solution 1

Turned advisor outreach into trackable leads, and lead data into business insight

Leads reached EastWest Ageas through several routes:

  1. Website
  2. Bank branches (bancassurance advisors)
  3. Fields- offline (agents)
  4. Social media (in-app communication referrals)

Advisors relied on referrals and conversations on platforms like Facebook, and there was no consistent way to capture interest or trace a lead back to its source. The business couldn’t see which channels or advisors were producing results.

Design decision

Every lead, from any route, becomes a profile in the CRM with its source attached automatically. Each advisor gets a unique URL and QR code tied to their name, so when a prospect scans and submits, the lead is created and credited to that advisor with no manual logging. The website, bancassurance, agent, and social media sources are tagged along with the agent code.

Result

Advisors could reach prospects remotely, and every lead was tracked through the sales process. The business could now see where leads came from and compare advisor performance. The data showed that most leads came from Facebook, the main channel for advisor-customer relationships, which confirmed where to focus outreach and product opportunities.

From an online quote or an adviser’s digital calling card, prospects can submit a lead, get connected with an adviser, and continue through account setup, with progress tracked in our sales app (CRM).

Lead Applications & Product Comparison

Solution 2

Cut application time from 90 to 30 minutes

  • Complex applications were hard to complete, and advisors lacked a clear, consistent way to explain insurance options to customers.
  • Most advisors work on a tablet or phone, but the legacy platform wasn’t built for them. Support tickets repeatedly said it was hard to use on a tablet or phone, and that the system gave no clear error messages, so advisors didn’t know why they couldn’t proceed or what to do next.

Design decision

  • Simplified the application process and made it mobile-friendly.
  • Created a scannable product comparison that focused on benefits and amounts, so advisors could explain them consistently.
  • Added contextual help to guide advisors through the application process.
  • Built a quote calculator into the platform so advisors can assess a customer’s needs and quote on the spot.

Result

Advisors could go from needs assessment to quote to application in one place, on the device they use with customers. Application time dropped 67%. The comparison experience gave advisors a more consistent way to explain product benefits and amounts.

Optimized lead profile creation for advisors and customers, with guided forms, clearer product information, and contextual help to reduce the burden of a complex insurance application.

EWAPP (CRM)

Solution 3

Gave sales teams one view of every customer and their next step

Advisors needed to manage customer records, activity, applications, and follow-up across disconnected tools. Teams had limited visibility into customer progress.

Design decision

Bring customer records, activity, and next actions into one CRM, connected to the customer portal and the back-office application. We designed it around the application stages, so every lead shows exactly which step they’re on. This lets teams see:

  • Which customers are in which stage of the application process
  • What documents are needed and what’s missing
  • How long each lead takes to get through the application, so slow stages are visible.
  • Whether a customer is progressing on their own or still relying on an advisor. Most customers lean heavily on advisors, so this shows how ready they are for self-service.

Result

Updates reflected across connected systems, customers could check application status in the portal, and advisors had clearer visibility into progress and follow-up.

Customer Profile

The legacy customer profile was difficult to use across screen sizes and needed to fit the newer platform experience.

Design decision

Modernize the profile as a responsive web experience, aligning it with the new visual system and improving its information hierarchy.

Result

The profile became a more consistent, responsive experience for staff working with customer and policy information.

Customers and advisors had a clearer view of application progress and relevant policy information, reducing uncertainty as applications moved through review.

Bring status, documents, and policy information into the broader digital service experience, reducing reliance on disconnected follow-ups.

Self-service Payments

Solution 6

Let customers pay premiums digitally and see it confirmed instantly

Customers needed a straightforward way to pay premiums digitally and know whether payment had gone through.

Design decision

Add credit card and selected digital wallet options, with each payment reflected in the customer’s account in real time, so confirmation is immediate and doesn’t need a follow-up.

Result

Customers could pay premiums through digital channels and see the payment reflected in their accounts instantly.

EWA Design System

Building foundations for scale

Built the first design system for EastWest Ageas

As the number of platforms grew, teams needed consistent patterns and a more efficient way to design and implement new experiences.

Design decision

Establish shared design foundations, components, specifications, and accessibility guidance, then support adoption through education and phased modernization.

Creating a shared visual language

We established reusable foundations and components to support the new digital experiences and reduce inconsistencies across platforms.

Bringing accessibility into the process

I introduced accessibility considerations into our design foundations, component specifications, and design reviews. This gave design, engineering, and QA shared expectations for creating more inclusive web experiences.

Modernized while reducing legacy debt

In alignment with Product Owners, the Head of Digital Transformation, Scrum Masters, engineering, and QA, we established a phased strategy for shipping new experiences while auditing, cleaning up, and progressively replacing legacy components. Reusable patterns, Adobe XD to Figma libraries, and regular design-system education helped teams modernize without pausing delivery.

Faster Design Hand-offs

Shared components and clearer specifications reduced production handoff cycles by giving design, engineering, and QA a shared source of truth.

Adoption was part of the product

Our team ran fortnightly Design System 101 sessions with engineering, QA, and product owners.

Learnings

Transformation reinforced an important principle in my approach to product design: meaningful experiences emerge when customer needs, business operations, and technology are designed together.

Digital transformation is not just translating paper forms to digital

The biggest opportunities came from redesigning operational workflows, handoffs, and business processes behind the interface rather than the screens themselves.

Cross-functional alignment is as important as design execution

Early collaboration with executives, operations, product, engineering, compliance, and external consultants reduced rework and accelerated decision-making throughout the program.

Product strategy requires balancing ambition with business reality

A phased modernization approach allowed us to improve existing experiences while progressing toward a more connected ecosystem. The ability to prioritize what could be improved now versus what needed longer-term investment was essential.

Designing for the whole journey, not only screens

Improving one touchpoint in isolation would have created downstream bottlenecks. Mapping the relationships between CRM, underwriting, onboarding, and back-office systems was critical to delivering meaningful impact.

Design leadership is partly about creating clarity.

When multiple designers and teams are working across interconnected products, my role was not to make every decision myself, but to establish the principles, priorities, and context that allowed the team to make good decisions independently.

Small data quality improvements can create significant business value

Reducing manual entry, improving form validation, and increasing visibility into application statuses had a direct impact on underwriting speed and policy issuance outcomes.

Building organizational design maturity scales impact beyond a single project

Introducing design systems, design reviews, Figma workflows, and design sprint practices helped create a foundation that future teams could build upon long after the transformation was completed.
