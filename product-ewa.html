Eastwest Ageas

Financial Services / Insurance (2023-2024)

EastWest Ageas operated across multiple digital platforms supporting different stages of the insurance journey. As the business expanded its digital capabilities, independently developed systems created fragmented workflows for customers, financial advisors, and internal operations teams.

The challenge was bigger than improving individual interfaces. It required connecting the experiences and operational processes behind them without disrupting the business-critical workflows already supporting the organization.

I led the design direction across the ecosystem while remaining hands-on in research, workflow design, interaction design, and key product experiences.

About

Context

EastWest Ageas is a life insurance firm formed in 2015 by EastWest Bank, one of the largest banks in the Philippines, and Ageas Group, a multinational insurance company headquarted in Brussels operates in 13 countries worldwide.

Challenge

Problem

One ecosystem. Multiple disconnected experiences.

Each platform supported a different part of the insurance journey from agents and branch teams managing sales, to operations teams processing policies, to customers managing their coverage easily.

These experiences had evolved across different teams and systems, creating different workflows, interaction patterns, and operational dependencies.

We needed to design around the specific needs of each user group while creating a more connected and consistent experience across the ecosystem.

Main bottleneck

Information was fragmented. Financial advisors relied on manual processes, spreadsheets, repeated data entry, and handoffs between applications. What should have been one continuous insurance journey often felt like several disconnected processes.

Balancing legacy improvements with future platform strategy

How could we modernize the ecosystem while maintaining business continuity?

The challenge wasn't simply designing better screens. It was deciding where design effort would create the most impact.

We needed to improve existing, business-critical workflows while also shaping a more scalable experience for future platforms.

The central challenge was to create a more connected insurance ecosystem without disrupting the operations that kept the business running.

Findings

Key Insights

The biggest friction wasn't within the interfaces. It was between systems and teams.

Through interviews, focus groups, remote usability testing, and in-branch observations, we examined how customers, advisors, and operations teams moved through the insurance journey.

Tools Used

01 - Fragmented handoffs slowed down the insurance journey

Customer information moved between disconnected platforms, creating repeated data entry, manual coordination, and unclear ownership between teams. The friction wasn't isolated to a single screen. It accumulated across the journey.

02 - Advisors managed systems instead of customers

Financial advisors navigated multiple tools to manage prospects, complete applications, compare products, and follow up with customers. The fragmented workflow made routine sales activities harder to manage and track.

03 - Legacy product configuration limited business agility (4 months time to market)

Creating or updating insurance products required costly configuration within SAP. These dependencies increased maintenance overhead and made it harder to introduce new offerings efficiently.

The opportunity was to improve the entire operational journey, not simply digitize existing forms.

A fragmented insurance ecosystem was slowing down both customers, advisors, and internal teams.

Research & Alignment

Cross-functional collaboration

Stakeholder alignment around product direction

I worked closely with Product Owners, QA, engineers, business analysts, sales, and department heads to translate the broader product direction into clear product design priorities.

Iterative Process

We worked in short, collaborative design sprints within an agile development cycle, moving from research and framing to prototyping, testing, and refinement. Each sprint turned feedback into focused improvements while keeping design, product, and engineering aligned.

I also helped build buy-in from senior stakeholders, including the CEO, VP of Sales, and Head of Digital Transformation, by connecting design decisions to business goals, operational needs, and customer outcomes.

Making the strategic decision

Strategy

How do we deliver given the time constraints and resources available as we migrate our tools, improve design and development process?

We needed to balance immediate usability improvements with long-term platform strategy.

We considered three options for improving the ecosystem:

01 – Evidence

Address immediate usability and operational problems within the current platforms.

02 – Options

Rebuild the underlying systems as a complete replacement.

03 – Modernize Progressively

Improve critical workflows while introducing new platform experiences and progressively replacing legacy components.

We chose to modernize progressively.

We focused on improving the most critical workflows while introducing new platform experiences and progressively replacing legacy components. This approach allowed us to improve usability and operational efficiency without disrupting business-critical processes.

Remote Selling & Digital Lead Acquisition

Solution 1

Taking insurance beyond the branch.

We extended that familiar way of connecting into a digital sales journey.

Advisors (bancassurance and sales agents) could share a QR code through through existing communication channels, giving prospects to access a lead form and continue the insurance journey.

  • Advisors (bancassurance and sales agents) have their own unique url and generated QR code where leads can access a lead form.
  • Each lead is tracked and managed through our own new CRM (Sales App).
  • Advisors could follow up with prospects and complete the insurance journey remotely.
  • Operations teams could process applications and issue policies without losing context between handoffs.

Make digital acquisition easier while preserving customer context as leads moved into the sales process.

This approach has led to an increase in qualified leads and improvement in conversion rates.

From an online quote or an adviser’s QR code, prospects can submit a lead, get connected with an adviser, and continue through account setup, with progress tracked in EWAPP.

EWA Design System

Building foundations for scale

Establishing Design System

As the number of digital platforms grew, consistency and implementation efficiency became increasingly important.

I founded and scaled EastWest Ageas's first design system across the new platforms. Shared foundations and components gave product, engineering, and QA a common reference as the transformation expanded.

Creating a shared visual language

We established reusable foundations and components to support the new digital experiences and reduce inconsistencies across platforms.

Bringing accessibility into the process

I introduced accessibility considerations into our design foundations, component specifications, and design reviews. This gave design, engineering, and QA shared expectations for creating more inclusive web experiences.

Modernized while reducing legacy debt

With alignment with Product Owners, Head of Digital Transformation, Scrum Masters, Lead Engineer, and Lead QAs we've created a phased strategy for shipping new experiences while auditing, cleaning up, and progressively replacing legacy components. Reusable patterns, Adobe XD to Figma libraries, and regular design-system education helped teams modernize without pausing delivery.

Shared components and clearer specifications reduced production handoff cycles by giving design, engineering, and QA a shared source of truth.

Learnings

Transformation reinforced an important principle in my approach to product design: meaningful experiences emerge when customer needs, business operations, and technology are designed together.

Digital transformation is not just translating paper forms to digital

The biggest opportunities came from redesigning operational workflows, handoffs, and business processes behind the interface rather than the screens themselves.

Cross-functional alignment is as important as design execution

Early collaboration with executives, operations, product, engineering, compliance, and external consultants reduced rework and accelerated decision-making throughout the program.

Product strategy requires balancing ambition with business reality

phased modernization approach allowed us to improve existing experiences while progressing toward a more connected ecosystem. The ability to prioritize what could be improved now versus what needed longer-term investment was essential.

Designing for the whole journey, not only screens

Improving one touchpoint in isolation would have created downstream bottlenecks. Mapping the relationships between CRM, underwriting, onboarding, and back-office systems was critical to delivering meaningful impact.

Design leadership is partly about creating clarity.

When multiple designers and teams are working across interconnected products, my role was not to make every decision myself, but to establish the principles, priorities, and context that allowed the team to make good decisions independently.

Small data quality improvements can create significant business value

Reducing manual entry, improving form validation, and increasing visibility into application statuses had a direct impact on underwriting speed and policy issuance outcomes.

Building organizational design maturity scales impact beyond a single project

Introducing design systems, design reviews, Figma workflows, and design sprint practices helped create a foundation that future teams could build upon long after the transformation was completed.
