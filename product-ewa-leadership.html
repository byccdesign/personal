The leadership challenge

Keep today moving while designing what comes next

The transformation touched sales, customer service, policy operations, and the systems connecting them. Teams needed to improve legacy workflows while researching, testing, and designing new platforms. The central leadership challenge was deciding what to address now, what to validate, and how to keep parallel workstreams moving toward one coherent experience.

Set a north star, then make it actionable

Translate product direction into design priorities

I helped anchor the team around a shared north star: a connected, scalable, and intuitive insurance experience across the ecosystem. I translated that direction into focused workstreams across platforms, clarified ownership, and assigned responsibilities so designers could move forward in parallel without losing sight of the whole journey.

Prioritize progress on two horizons

I balanced improvements to existing experiences with discovery for the new platforms. We addressed high-friction workflows while using research and usability testing to validate larger changes before they moved into delivery. This phased approach let the team keep improving the current experience as we built toward the future one.

Give the team ownership and a clear rhythm

Turn broad goals into focused sprint work

I organized design responsibilities around platform and journey workstreams, making ownership and dependencies easier to understand. In collaborative design sprints, the team moved from framing and research to prototyping, testing, and refinement. Regular critiques and working sessions kept the work visible and gave designers room to contribute while maintaining a consistent direction.

Make progress and decisions visible

Weekly design updates gave stakeholders a clear view of progress, risks, research findings, and upcoming decisions. I used those conversations to resolve open questions early and keep design, product, and delivery teams aligned on the next useful step.

Build alignment across the organization

Connect design decisions to shared outcomes

I worked closely with Product Owners, engineers, QA, business analysts, sales, and department heads to translate broad product goals into workable design priorities. With senior leaders, including the CEO and business heads, I framed design proposals around customer needs, operational realities, and the direction of the business.

Workshops and design reviews gave teams a place to surface constraints, test assumptions, and agree on the trade-offs behind a decision. That shared context helped build buy-in and reduced the distance between product intent and implementation.

Create practices that outlast a project

Build the shared design foundation

I established EastWest Ageas’s first design system across the new platforms, bringing reusable patterns, accessibility expectations, and clearer handoff guidance into the team’s everyday work. Moving the component library from Adobe XD to Figma gave design, engineering, and QA a shared reference as the ecosystem grew.

Make adoption part of the work

Fortnightly Design System 101 sessions helped product owners, engineering, and QA understand and apply the foundations. Alongside phased legacy improvements, these practices allowed teams to modernize without stopping delivery.

How I lead design

My role was to make the direction clear enough for teams to act on, while staying close enough to the work to understand what needed to change. I see design leadership as the practice of creating shared context, making thoughtful trade-offs, and helping people do their best work together.

Set direction without losing the detail

I connected strategy to research, journey decisions, and interface work, so the north star stayed useful in day-to-day design choices.

Make ownership and priorities clear

Focused workstreams and sprint rhythms helped the design team contribute across platforms while moving toward a common experience.

Earn alignment through collaboration

I built stakeholder confidence by making trade-offs visible and connecting design decisions to customer, business, and operational needs.

Leave the team with stronger foundations

The design system, accessibility practices, and shared review habits gave teams a more repeatable way to build and improve the ecosystem.
