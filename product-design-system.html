Background

Bits.Freelancer.com is Freelancer.com's official design system that provides reusable UI components, design patterns, accessibility guidelines, and front-end foundations used across Freelancer.com products and its family of companies.

Built using Angular and shared component libraries, Bits serves as the bridge between product design and engineering, enabling teams to build consistent experiences across web and native applications.

Cross-platform Design System

The system supported web, native apps, internal tools, and multiple product ecosystems, which meant component decisions needed to balance product flexibility with shared standards.

Context and Constraints

A migration exposed the cost of inconsistency

Freelancer.com was undergoing a large-scale migration of its marketplace platform while multiple product teams continued shipping new features. The design system needed to serve live product work, legacy clean-up, and future platform standards at the same time.

The important part is not only that components were created. The work required auditing ambiguity, aligning cross-functional teams, documenting decisions, and creating a system that product teams could actually adopt under delivery pressure.

Before

  • Product teams moved quickly without scalable UI governance
  • Similar components were redesigned or rebuilt in different areas
  • Legacy patterns made UI behavior harder to predict
  • Design files and implementation details were not always aligned
  • Accessibility expectations varied between product surfaces
  • Documentation did not answer common usage questions

After

  • Shared components reduced duplicated design and engineering effort
  • Usage guidelines clarified when and how to apply patterns
  • Accessibility requirements became part of component foundations
  • Design reviews helped teams catch inconsistencies earlier
  • Figma libraries and Angular components moved closer together
  • Product teams had a stronger foundation for migration work

Strategic Approach

UX Audit: Understanding the System

find_in_page

Audited legacy UI elements, reusable components, and old interaction patterns

Before creating new components, I conducted a comprehensive audit of existing interfaces to identify duplication, inconsistencies, and opportunities for standardization.

This became the foundation for planning future component architecture and prioritizing migration efforts.

I've audited and mapped existing UI patterns across the platform to establish a scalable component architecture shared between design, engineering, and QA teams.

Prioritization

Turning audit findings into a practical roadmap

The audit uncovered more inconsistencies than the team could address at once, so the work needed a prioritization model. I helped group patterns by product reach, implementation effort, accessibility risk, and migration value so the team could focus on components that would unlock the most reuse.

cycleReuse Potential

Prioritized components that appeared across multiple product flows, especially forms, cards, navigation, modals, and status patterns.

Accessibility

Accessibility & Internationalisation

Freelancer.com serves millions of users worldwide, making accessibility and inclusivity essential. When designing components, we considered diverse languages, cultural contexts, and accessibility needs to ensure our products could be used effectively by as many people as possible.

These standards helped teams think through keyboard access, responsive layouts, text expansion, localization, contrast, and component states before patterns were reused at scale.

Component Decisions

Designing components as product decisions, not just UI assets

Each component needed clear anatomy, states, responsive behavior, accessibility notes, content guidance, and examples of appropriate use. The goal was to reduce interpretation at the point of implementation so teams could move faster without creating new inconsistencies.

Inputs

  • Legacy UI audit findings
  • Recurring product team requests
  • Engineering implementation constraints
  • Accessibility and internationalisation needs
  • QA feedback from shipped product surfaces

Outputs

  • Reusable Figma components
  • Angular component guidance
  • Usage rules and examples
  • Documented states and variants
  • Review-ready implementation notes

Learnings

corporate_fare

A design system is an organizational product.

Design systems are not just collections of UI components; they are strategic assets that drive consistency and efficiency across an organization.

menu_book

Documentation is as important as the component itself.

Comprehensive documentation ensures that the design system is maintainable and scalable over time. Having a clear guidance improves adoption more than perfect visuals.

accessibility_new

Accessibility scales better when built into foundations.

Integrating accessibility considerations from the ground up ensures that all users can effectively interact with the design system, regardless of their abilities or the devices they use.

tune

Design systems should be flexible and adaptable.

Design systems must evolve with the changing needs of users and the organization. Flexibility allows for innovation while maintaining consistency.
